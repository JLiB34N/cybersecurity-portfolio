## 5. Learning Journey

My journey in cybersecurity has helped me understand that protecting computer systems requires more than just knowing how to use security tools. It involves understanding network communication, identifying abnormal activities, investigating potential threats and applying appropriate security measures.

One of the important topics I learned during this course was **network protocols and network communication**. Understanding how devices communicate through TCP/IP helped me develop a stronger foundation in networking. In particular, learning about TCP connection establishment improved my understanding of the three-way handshake, which consists of SYN, SYN-ACK and ACK packets. I applied this knowledge in my Wireshark project by capturing packets generated from a local Python web server. By examining the source and destination ports, TCP flags, sequence numbers and acknowledgement numbers, I could observe how a connection was established in practice. This also helped me understand how attackers could abuse the TCP connection process through SYN flood attacks.

Another important area was **network security monitoring and threat detection**, which is also covered in Cisco's CyberOps Associate training. I learned that cybersecurity analysts need to examine different sources of security information, including network traffic, system logs and security alerts. These sources help analysts identify suspicious activities and investigate potential security incidents. Concepts such as Security Information and Event Management (SIEM), network intrusion analysis and incident response have helped me understand how security operations teams monitor and respond to threats. My Nmap project also allowed me to apply some of these ideas by identifying open ports and potentially vulnerable services on Metasploitable 2. It demonstrated how reconnaissance can help security professionals understand the exposure of a network.

One challenge I encountered was during Week 6, when we were required to ping computers between different islands in a network exercise. Initially, we could not even successfully ping computers within our own island. After the lecturer investigated the issue, two possible causes were identified. First, the computers' firewall settings were blocking ICMP echo requests. Second, Apple operating systems have different network and firewall settings compared with Windows, which affected the connectivity tests. This experience taught me that unsuccessful network communication does not necessarily mean that the network configuration is incorrect. Firewall policies and operating system differences must also be considered when troubleshooting connectivity problems.

Another area I still find challenging is using Docker through command-line instructions. Since Docker is relatively new to me, I sometimes struggle to remember the commands and their parameters. However, through repeated practice and hands-on lab exercises, I believe I can gradually become more confident in using containers and configuring Docker networks.

Moving forward, my main professional development goal is to obtain the **Cisco CCNA certification by December 2027**. I will measure my progress by completing the required preparation, practising network configuration exercises and ultimately passing the official CCNA examination. I also plan to continue developing my cybersecurity knowledge through the CyberOps Associate training, with a target completion date of March 2027.

Overall, this course has strengthened my interest in networking and cybersecurity. More importantly, it has taught me to approach technical problems systematically, investigate the underlying causes and continuously improve my practical skills.
